AI agent for small business: what it does in a working week

An AI agent for small business, followed through one working week: the tasks it takes, the ones that stay human, and what it costs to run.

An AI agent for a small business is software that reads incoming messages and documents, decides the next step against rules you set, and acts inside your tools: it replies, books appointments, updates the CRM or drafts a quote. In a company of five to twenty people it typically takes the first touch on routine enquiries and the admin around them, while discounts, complaints and anything that commits money stay with a person. Running costs are usually small next to the build, but since October 2026 they include per-message WhatsApp fees that used to be free.

Monday to Friday: what the agent actually handles

Monday morning is the weekend backlog: WhatsApp chats, contact-form entries and Instagram messages that arrived after Friday evening. By the time the team logs in, the agent has answered the ones it can answer from your price list, opening hours and delivery zones, tagged each lead by product and location, and left a short queue of the rest with a one-line summary on top of each.

Tuesday and Wednesday are follow-up days. Quotes sent the week before get a polite check-in on day three, written in the language the customer used, and every reply is logged against the deal. Appointment-based businesses such as clinics, salons, workshops and tuition centres let the agent offer free slots from the live calendar and send the reminder the evening before.

Thursday is documents. Supplier invoices arriving by email are read, matched to purchase orders and pushed into the accounting system as drafts for a person to approve. For Malaysian companies inside the LHDN e-invoice rollout, this is the step where a missing buyer tax identification number gets caught before month-end instead of after it.

Friday is reporting: a plain summary of the week sent to the owner, covering enquiries received, average response time, quotes still open and every conversation the agent handed to a human, with the reason it did so.

What stays human, and where to draw the line

The agent should not agree a discount, accept a refund, promise a delivery date it cannot see in your stock system or handle a complaint past the first acknowledgement. These are the moments where a wrong answer costs a customer, and where a Muslim-owned brand in particular is judged on whether it keeps its word.

The working rule we use with clients: if an action moves money, changes a commitment or involves an upset customer, the agent drafts and a person sends. Everything else it can complete on its own, with a log your team can read. Writing these rules down before the build takes about half a day with the owner and whoever answers WhatsApp today.

What it costs to run each month

Build cost depends on how many systems the agent has to touch, and our guide to what an AI agent costs breaks that down. Running cost has three parts that small businesses tend to underestimate.

The first is the language model. According to OpenAI's published API pricing, GPT-5.4 mini costs USD 0.75 per million input tokens and USD 4.50 per million output tokens. A support conversation of a few thousand tokens therefore costs a fraction of a US cent in model fees; long document reads and high volume are what move the bill.

The second is messaging. Meta's developer documentation states that from 1 October 2026 WhatsApp service messages, the replies sent inside the customer service window, are charged per message at each market's utility rate, after being free since November 2024. Meta's own Business Agent is billed separately at USD 2.00 per million tokens, which Meta puts at about 4 to 5 US cents for a typical message. For a business answering a few thousand WhatsApp messages a month, messaging can now cost more than the model.

The third is upkeep: someone updating the price list, FAQs and rules the agent reads from, and reviewing a sample of its conversations each week. Plan for a couple of hours a week from an existing team member. Without a named owner, answers drift out of date as prices and stock change.

How to roll out an AI agent for small business in 30 days

In week one, pick one channel and one job, usually first replies on WhatsApp or the website form, and export three months of real conversations to see which questions repeat. In week two, write the rules for what the agent may do alone, connect it to the calendar or CRM it needs, and test it against those past conversations. In week three, run it live with a person approving every reply. In week four, let it send on its own for the categories it handled correctly, keep approval on the rest, and compare response time and booked appointments with the month before.

Starting with one channel keeps the cost readable and the failures visible. Email, Instagram or invoice handling come after the first job has run cleanly for a full month.

Checklist before you sign with a provider

  • Ask which actions the agent can take without approval, and get that list written into the scope of work.
  • Confirm who pays the WhatsApp and model usage bills, and ask for an estimate based on your actual monthly message volume rather than a flat unlimited promise.
  • Check where conversation data is stored and that consent and retention meet the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 for your Malaysian customers.
  • Make sure every conversation the agent hands over arrives with a summary, so your team never asks the customer to repeat themselves.
  • Agree a weekly review of sample conversations for the first two months, with a named person on each side.

Questions small business owners ask

  • Can an AI agent reply in Malay, English and Arabic? — Yes. Current models handle all three, and the agent should answer in the language the customer wrote in. Product names, prices and halal certification details must come from your own data, never from the model.
  • Will customers know they are talking to an AI? — They should. A short line in the first reply saying it is an automated assistant, with a way to reach a person, protects trust and costs nothing.
  • Do I need the WhatsApp Business API? — For an agent that works around the clock and writes into your CRM, yes. The free WhatsApp Business app is built for a person on a phone, and connecting it to outside systems is limited.
  • Is it worth it below 50 enquiries a week? — Often not as a full agent. Well-written quick replies and a simple CRM may cover that volume until it grows.
