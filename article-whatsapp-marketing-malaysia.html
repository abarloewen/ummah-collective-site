WhatsApp marketing in Malaysia: broadcasts, automation and ads

WhatsApp marketing in Malaysia, compared: broadcasts, automation and click-to-WhatsApp ads, opt-in under PDPA, Meta's pricing and what to measure.

WhatsApp marketing in Malaysia works when it is built on permission and on conversations, not on mass blasts. In practice you choose between three tools: broadcasts to people who opted in, automation that replies and follows up for you, and click-to-WhatsApp ads that start new conversations. Most SMEs need all three, in that order of difficulty, and the order you build them in decides whether the channel grows or gets your number restricted.

Broadcasts, automation and ads: what each one actually does

Broadcasts are outbound messages to an existing list. On the free WhatsApp Business app, broadcast lists only reach people who have saved your number in their contacts, which keeps them small and personal. On the WhatsApp Business Platform (the API), you send approved message templates to any customer who has opted in, at a per-message cost set by Meta. Broadcasts are best for announcements, restocks, Ramadan and Hari Raya offers, and reminders to people who already know you.

Automation handles the conversations that broadcasts and ads create: instant first replies, qualifying questions, booking links, order status, follow-ups after two days of silence, and a clean handover to a person when the question needs judgement. This is where most of the revenue is won or lost, because a lead that waits hours for a reply rarely converts.

Click-to-WhatsApp ads run on Facebook and Instagram and open a chat with your business instead of a landing page. They are how you reach people who are not on your list yet. They also carry a pricing advantage: according to Meta's developer documentation, when a customer messages you from one of these ads and you reply within 24 hours, a free entry point window opens and all messages, including templates, are free for 72 hours.

Opt-in, PDPA and the rules that protect your number

Two sets of rules apply at the same time. Meta's own opt-in policy says a business must clearly state that the person is opting in to receive messages, must clearly name the business they will hear from, and must comply with applicable law. Meta also asks businesses to explain how to opt out of specific categories of messages and to honour those requests.

In Malaysia, the applicable law is the Personal Data Protection Act 2010. Under section 43 of the Act, an individual can require a business to stop processing their personal data for direct marketing. In practice that means three things: collect the number with a clear purpose at the point of capture, keep a record of when and where consent was given, and make stopping as easy as typing STOP.

The commercial reason matters as much as the legal one. Every message you send to someone who did not ask for it is a chance for them to block or report you, and that feedback affects the quality of your number. A smaller list of people who chose you will outperform a bought list every time.

What WhatsApp marketing costs to run

There are three cost layers. The first is Meta's messaging fee. Since 1 July 2025, according to Meta's developer documentation, the platform charges per message rather than per conversation: every marketing template is charged, while replies inside an open customer service window are free, and utility templates sent inside that window are free too. Meta publishes the rates by country and, since April 2026, also bills in Malaysian ringgit, so check the current rate card before you plan a campaign.

The second layer is the software: a provider dashboard or a custom integration that holds your templates, inbox, contact tags and automations. The third is the work itself: writing templates that get approved, designing the flows, connecting WhatsApp to your CRM and reviewing results every week. Businesses that budget only for the first layer usually end up with a busy inbox and no system behind it.

WhatsApp marketing in Malaysia: a 30-day setup plan

Week one is consent and capture. Add a WhatsApp opt-in to your website forms, checkout and Google Business Profile, with one sentence explaining what people will receive. Tag every contact by source so you know which entry point brings buyers.

Week two is the reply layer. Build the instant first reply, three to five qualifying questions, and the handover rule to a person. Test it in Bahasa Melayu and English, and in Chinese if your customers write in it.

Week three is follow-up. Add a reminder for leads who went quiet, a review request after delivery, and a re-engagement message for customers who have not bought in 90 days. Week four is the first paid test: a small click-to-WhatsApp campaign aimed at one offer, so the automation you built can carry the volume.

Checklist before you send your first campaign

  • Every contact on the list opted in with your business name visible, and you can show when and where.
  • Opt-out works in one word, is honoured automatically, and removes the contact from future marketing templates.
  • Someone owns replies during business hours, and the automation tells customers clearly when a person will answer.
  • Every chat is logged in your CRM with its source, so a sale can be traced back to the broadcast or ad that started it.
  • You measure replies, booked calls or orders and revenue per message sent, not just delivered and read rates.

Frequently asked questions

  • Can I use the free WhatsApp Business app for marketing? — Yes, for small lists of people who saved your number. Once you need automation, several team members on one number, or CRM integration, the WhatsApp Business Platform is the better fit.
  • Is it legal to send WhatsApp promotions in Malaysia? — It is, when people have agreed to receive them and can stop at any time. The PDPA gives individuals the right to require you to stop using their data for direct marketing, and Meta requires a clear opt-in.
  • Should I buy a contact list to grow faster? — No. Bought lists break Meta's opt-in rules, invite blocks and reports that hurt your number, and rarely convert. Build the list from your own customers, site and ads.
  • How often can I send broadcasts? — There is no fixed number that suits every business. Send when you have something useful to say, and watch blocks and opt-outs after each send as your signal to slow down.
